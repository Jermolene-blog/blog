# food fact-check log (batch 2)

Input: q2/food.json (70 items). Output: checked4/food.json (66 items). 6 fixes, 4 drops. WebSearch calls: 6. Direct Wikipedia fetches were refused unless the URL had first appeared in a search result, so only searched-for pages could be checked. No near-duplicates of existing/food.txt (the Cornish pasty, Stilton, Gloucester cheese, pease pudding and Smith's-related items in existing ask about different facts).

## Fixes
- Battenberg: early Battenbergs had nine squares, and some had as many as 25, so "Nine" was arguably correct. Changed to "A modern Battenberg" and replaced "Nine" with "Eight". Source: https://huffingtonpost.co.uk/entry/battenberg-cake-original-recipe-history_uk_66f680c2e4b0632d68f673c0
- Vimto: Wikipedia gives both Salford (intro/infobox) and Manchester as the 1908 birthplace, so a city answer is open to challenge. Changed it to ask for the county: Lancashire (in 1908 both places were in Lancashire); distractors Yorkshire, Cheshire, Staffordshire. Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vimto
- Gordon Ramsay: he was never signed by Rangers. He was a trialist only, so "on the books" was wrong. Reworded to "had trials with which football club?" Source: https://www.goal.com/en/news/which-football-club-did-gordon-ramsay-play-for-behind-chefs/1qbpm93jfk0wt12gdgdcmq4m96
- Smith's crisps: confirmed a twist of salt from 1920. The distractors "A little glass bottle" and "A glass vial" were near-identical, so the vial became "A cardboard tube". Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Smiths_(snack_foods)
- Haggis/Burns Night: haggis is also eaten on St Andrew's Night and at Hogmanay, so "centrepiece of a supper" was open to challenge. Reworded to "ceremonially addressed at a supper", which only fits Burns Night. Wording only.
- Babycham: replaced "hugely popular in the 1960s" with the softer "a 1960s favourite" so the question does not rest on an unsourced claim about popularity. Wording only.

## Verified (risky)
- Rock salmon = dogfish/huss (small sharks). Coley, pollock and whiting are not listed. Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rock_salmon
- Walkers: founded by Henry Walker in Leicester, making crisps from 1948. Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Walkers_(snack_foods), https://www.gracesguide.co.uk/Walkers_Crisps
- The other kept items are plain encyclopaedia facts with one defensible answer: ingredients, accompaniments, current places of manufacture, long-running slogans and mascots, Mrs Beeton, the Cradocks and the Two Fat Ladies. I left them unsourced because the search budget was used up.
